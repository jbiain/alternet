# 001 — Alternet Manifesto (draft)

## Why

The internet was built so anyone could take part. Today most people only consume it. Publishing,
mail, chat and now AI run through a few large companies, behind accounts, fees, rules and
regulation we don't control. Every person already owns a capable computer, and almost none of
them use it to take part in the network.

Alternet is an attempt to make participation ordinary again, in the spirit of the BBS era: you
run your own board, your own pages, your own mail and your own models, and you can connect with
others without asking anyone's permission.

## Principles

1. **Participants, not spectators.** Every node can publish and serve, not only consume.
2. **Ownership.** Your keys, your data, your names. No account on someone else's server is required.
3. **No mandatory center.** Any bootstrap or directory service is a convenience and must be replaceable.
4. **Works offline, degrades gracefully.** LAN, hotspot, USB stick and slow links are first-class. Always-online is never assumed.
5. **Nothing exposed by default.** Installing Alternet shares nothing until the owner chooses.
6. **Simple and open.** A small, publicly specified protocol that anyone can implement. Text first; a terminal is enough.
7. **Compose, don't reinvent.** Reuse proven standards and libraries wherever they fit.
8. **Honest limits.** We state what doesn't work (phones, NAT, hosting liability) instead of hiding it.

## Non-goals (for now)

- Replacing the web or the existing internet. Alternet coexists with it.
- Anonymity or censorship-proof guarantees.
- Payments, tokens, or a storage marketplace.
- Running one AI model across many home computers.
- A global naming system.

## Open decisions

- Licence (Apache 2.0 vs MPL/AGPL for the node software).
- First audience (see 002).
- Foundation transport and language (see 005, after the Phase 0 spike).
